### Key findings

- **The judge essentially never predicts "ambiguous": 0% recall on the ambiguous gold label** (0 of 25 correctly classified as ambiguous). Per the rubric prompt, the judge is being forced into match-or-divergence on genuinely underspecified pairs rather than using the third option it's explicitly given -- 16 of the 25 ambiguous-gold pairs were called divergence outright. This is a real prompt-calibration gap, reported here rather than hidden, in the same spirit as `docs/judge_calibration.md`'s honestly-reported kappa.
- **The project's own canonical positive fixture (`NCT02872116`) was misclassified as `match`, not `divergence`.** This is the confirmed real example `discrepancy_definition.md` SS3 and `sprint_4_implementation.md` name as the S4-05/S4-08 canonical test case -- a miss on it here is the single most concrete signal that the current rubric prompt needs revision before S4-05 is built on top of it, not a footnote.
- Every miss in the **timeframe_reword** and **construct_swap** categories is 0 -- the two mechanically well-defined categories (a pure unit rewording; a completely different named construct) are both classified perfectly. The failures concentrate entirely in categories requiring judgment about materiality (population thresholds, primary/secondary framing, and all three ambiguous sub-categories), which is where a rubric revision should focus.

**Divergence fraction: 18.9%**, against Robinson & Goodman's raw registered-vs-current change rate of 31.7% and their own manually-reviewed true-change rate of ~8% (`discrepancy_definition.md` SS1/SS5 -- the ~8% figure is a sanity-check ballpark from a different therapeutic-area mix, not a hard target).

**18.9% is within a defensible range of the literature's ~8% true-change ballpark** (allowing for a different therapeutic-area mix and a much smaller cohort than the literature's own sample).

**Caveat on that number**: only 2 of 196 corpus pairs came back `ambiguous` here, consistent with the 100-pair set's own finding above that the judge rarely uses that label. Given 64% of the hand-labeled set's genuinely-ambiguous pairs were called `divergence` instead, some fraction of this 18.9% divergence figure is likely cases that should have landed in `ambiguous` (a human-review candidate) rather than a clean positive -- so this number should be read as an upper bound on "cases worth a human look," not a clean divergence rate, until the ambiguous-recall gap above is addressed.
